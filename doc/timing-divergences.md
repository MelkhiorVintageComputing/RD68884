# Clock counts against the MC68881

`CLAUDE.md` puts cycles per instruction after size, and nothing requires RD68884 to take the MC68881's clock counts. This document measures them anyway, against FPU section 8, so that every difference is known and explained. Bus *timing* is a different matter and is not negotiable: that is `doc/bus-timing.md`.

`make cycles` times 264 instructions on the system that FPU 8.5.1 assumes: an MC68020 (RD68021, at the Makefile's `RD68021_REV`, a738237) and the FPU on a 32-bit port, memory with no wait states. Each row is a regression check: its count is frozen in `tools/cycles.frozen`, and a change in either direction fails the target until someone looks at it and freezes it again (`make cycles FREEZE=1`). The tables below are generated from the measurement.

## What decides it

Three things, each visible in a column.

**The core runs at three times the bus clock.** The FPU's computation, measured in bus clocks, is a third of what it is in core clocks. That is where the transcendentals, the divide, the square root and packed decimal gain: compare the RD68884 column with the ISS column.

**At the same clock, the microcode is about as fast as the MC68881's.** The ISS column is in core clocks and includes an idealised dialogue. Against the manual's counts it is:
- 17 % slower on the register-to-register arithmetic: FADD 60 against 51, FDIV 130 against 103, FSQRT 115 against 107;
- level on the arithmetic from memory;
- 17 % faster on the transcendentals: FSIN 430 against 391, FETOX 370 against 497;
- much faster on packed decimal: 220 against 888 in, 1665 against 2002 out.

The datapath is one 72-bit adder and a 72 × 16 multiplier, chosen for size (`doc/size-and-speed.md`). Division and square root take a quotient bit a clock, multiplication four 16-bit steps. Packed decimal scales by a power of ten from the constant ROM, one multiplication or division (two multiplications beyond 10^±27), rather than converting digit by digit.

**Everything else is RD68021's.** In every row where the FPU ×30 count is close to the RD68884 count, the time is the main processor's. That means the register-to-register arithmetic, every memory operand, the control registers, FMOVEM, the conditionals, FSAVE and FRESTORE. `sys_tb +trace` shows it bus cycle by bus cycle.

RD68884 answers every interface-register access with no wait states. A CIR cycle is three clocks, response and save reads included; the manual assumes five for those (FPU 8.5.1). A primitive is always ready when it is read: the response the main processor finds after an operand transfer is the next one, not come-again. The extra time is all *between* bus cycles, in RD68021's microcode:

| Where | Clocks in RD68021 (a738237) | Before its coprocessor work (dda5ee6) | Example (`FADD.X (A0),FP1`) |
|---|---|---|---|
| Reading an evaluate-effective-address primitive, to the first operand read | 10 | 18 | response `$960C` ends at 1894, operand read at 1904 |
| Each long word of operand: memory read, operand-CIR write | 8 | 12 | 1904, 1912, 1920 |
| The last operand write, to the next response read | 3 | 7 | 1927 to 1930 |
| A null primitive, to the next instruction's first bus cycle | 5 to 9 | 9 to 15 | |

RD68021's coprocessor work (its branch `cp-speed`, on its master since a738237) was prompted by this table. It decodes more of each primitive at once, posts its CIR writes, and unrolls FMOVEM and four-byte transfers. Over the mix, the table's total went from 34,028 clocks to 29,935.

With a real MC68020 those rows would take the manual's protocol times plus RD68884's computation. That cannot be measured here, because the only MC68020 in this repository is RD68021.

## One clock for everything: the same-clock BIU

With RD68884's core on the main processor's own clock, the computation runs at the bus clock instead of three times it. The design point's advantage on long instructions goes, and the ISS column is roughly what remains. The same-clock BIU (`doc/bus-timing.md`) is for that board. Against the default BIU on the same clock, it saves what the synchronisers cost:

| Instruction | FPU 8 | same clock | asynchronous, core = bus | design point (core 3×) |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| `FMOVE.X FP0,FP1` | 33 | 43 | 43 | 23 |
| `FADD.X FP0,FP1` | 51 | 61 | 57 | 23 |
| `FADD.D (A0),FP1` | 80 | 95 | 100 | 54 |
| `FDIV.X FP0,FP1` | 103 | 134 | 134 | 53 |
| `FSIN.X FP0,FP1` | 391 | 431 | 433 | 153 |
| `FMOVE.L FPCR,D0` | 31 | 35 | 48 | 36 |
| `FMOVEM.X FP0-FP3,(A1)` | 139 | 129 | 183 | 135 |
| `FSAVE (A1), idle` | 54 | 84 | 115 | 91 |
| `FNOP` | 18 | 19 | 26 | 20 |
| `FBEQ.W, taken` | 20 | 27 | 34 | 28 |

- **Every interface-register access is now three clocks.** On the protocol-bound rows the same-clock BIU beats the design point, though its core is three times slower: FMOVEM, the control registers, FSAVE, the conditionals. FMOVEM.X of four registers out takes 129 clocks against the MC68881's 139.
- **Over the whole mix:** 73,021 clocks, against 74,208 for the asynchronous BIU at the bus clock and 106,733 for the MC68881 (the category table).

### The response hold-off

Measured with RD68021 at dda5ee6. On the same clock, the main processor's response read used to arrive before the sequencer had answered a command it had just taken. The read got `$8900`, come again, and RD68021 polled again, about eleven clocks later. Conditionals were the worst hit: FNOP took 33 clocks against 30 on the asynchronous BIU.

The BIU now withholds DSACK on such a read until the answer is there, which FPU 10.5 allows (`doc/bus-timing.md`, "The response hold-off"):
- it applies only once the sequencer has taken the command;
- it lasts at most `RESP_HOLD` clocks, 20 by default.

Measured on the raw times, before the calibration row is subtracted:

| Build | Clocks saved | Rows slower |
|---|---|---|
| Same clock | 1,524 | none |
| Default | 179 | none |

**One effect on the table above:** a row is its time less the calibration row's (a lone FNOP), and the hold-off makes that lone FNOP 10 clocks faster on the same clock, 37 against 47. An FNOP right behind a released computation gains nothing: the sequencer has not taken it yet, so it is answered come-again as before. Rows that end in such an FNOP therefore show up to 10 clocks more than before the hold-off, though in absolute time none got slower.

## The measurement

`tools/cycles.py gen` writes the program, `build/programs/cycles.S`. Every row is:

```
        <setup>                     operands into FP0 and FP1, A0 at the row's data
        fnop                        the FPU idle, as FPU 8.1 assumes
        .balignw 4,0x4E71           the timed code long-word aligned
        move.l  CNT,%d6             sys_tb's clock count, at RES+$88
        <the instruction>
        fnop                        waits for whatever the instruction released
        move.l  CNT,%d7
```

The row's count is D7 − D6 less the same with no instruction (the first row). The FNOP after the instruction is what puts a released computation's tail in the count: Table 8-2's times are the whole instruction, the part the MC68881 lets the main processor overlap included (FPU 8.2). Two other choices keep the counts stable:
- The alignment keeps each row's count independent of where the row lands. Shifting the whole program by a word moves no row.
- RD68021's instruction cache stays off, as Table 8-2 assumes: "instruction prefetches do not hit in the MC68020 cache (or it is disabled)" (FPU 8.5.1).

The operands are the "typical" ones of FPU 8.5.1: normal and in range. The source is 0.75, with 3 for FSCALE, FMOD and FREM, 2.75 for FINT and FINTRZ, and 3 in the integer column. FP1 is 1.75, or 100.25 for FMOD and FREM. The integer column of FACOS, FASIN and FATANH is left out, because no integer is in their domain but 0 and ±1. FMOVE out stores 3.140625, packed with k = 17.

The manual's numbers were read from the page images, not the text layer, whose OCR is unreliable for numerals.

`sys_tb +trace` prints every bus cycle with its first and last clock, and `tools/cycles.py gen OUT.S --only TEXT` writes a program with only the matching rows. Together they were how the table above was explained.

## The MC68882 build (RD68885)

`make cycles MODEL=68882` times the arithmetic and FMOVE rows on RD68885 (doc/rd68885.md) three ways, against FPU Table 8-3, the MC68882's times. The setup is that of the MC68881 measurement above: the design point, the FPU's core at three times the bus clock.
- **Total:** as above, the instruction and an FNOP after it.
- **Head:** the part of the instruction that overlaps a previous instruction's tail. It is measured after a FETOX, whose tail is longer than any head: FETOX's total plus the instruction's, less the two together.
- **Tail:** the part during which the main processor can go on: the total less its release, the count with no FNOP after it.

**What the numbers say:**
- **The totals** are about a quarter of Table 8-3's, as for the MC68881 above: the FPU's core runs at three times the bus clock.
- **Fully concurrent:** FMOVE in from S, D or X, FMOVE register to register, and FMOVE.X out have a head as long as their total. The CU completes them during the previous instruction's tail, as Table 8-3 shows (doc/rd68885.md, R4).
- **Partially concurrent:** the other instructions with a memory source have a head close to the table's. It is the dialog and the operand transfer, which the CU runs while the APU computes.

- **Single and double stores** have no head where the table gives 38 and 44. RD68885 keeps the MC68881's dialog for them (the plan; doc/rd68885.md).
