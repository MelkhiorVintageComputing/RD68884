# Clock counts against the MC68881

`CLAUDE.md` puts cycles per instruction after size, and nothing requires RD68884 to take the MC68881's clock counts. This document measures them anyway, against FPU section 8, so that every difference is known and explained. Bus *timing* is a different matter and is not negotiable: that is `doc/bus-timing.md`.

`make cycles` times 264 instructions on the system that FPU 8.5.1 assumes: an MC68020 (RD68021) and the FPU on a 32-bit port, memory with no wait states. Each row is a regression check: its count is frozen in `tools/cycles.frozen`, and a change in either direction fails the target until someone looks at it and freezes it again (`make cycles FREEZE=1`). The tables below are generated from the measurement.

## What decides it

Three things, each visible in a column.

**The core runs at three times the bus clock.** The FPU's computation, measured in bus clocks, is a third of what it is in core clocks. That is where the transcendentals, the divide, the square root and packed decimal gain: compare the RD68884 column with the ISS column.

**At the same clock, the microcode is about as fast as the MC68881's.** The ISS column is in core clocks and includes an idealised dialogue. Against the manual's counts it is:
- 13 % slower on the register-to-register arithmetic: FADD 60 against 51, FDIV 130 against 103, FSQRT 115 against 107;
- level on the arithmetic from memory;
- 17 % faster on the transcendentals: FSIN 430 against 391, FETOX 370 against 497;
- much faster on packed decimal: 220 against 888 in, 1665 against 2002 out.

The datapath is one 72-bit adder and a 72 × 16 multiplier, chosen for size (`doc/size-and-speed.md`). Division and square root take a quotient bit a clock, multiplication four 16-bit steps. Packed decimal scales by a power of ten from the constant ROM, one multiplication or division (two multiplications beyond 10^±27), rather than converting digit by digit.

**Everything else is RD68021's.** In every row where the FPU ×30 count is close to the RD68884 count, the time is the main processor's. That means the register-to-register arithmetic, every memory operand, the control registers, FMOVEM, the conditionals, FSAVE and FRESTORE. `sys_tb +trace` shows it bus cycle by bus cycle.

RD68884 answers every interface-register access with no wait states. A CIR cycle is three clocks, response and save reads included; the manual assumes five for those (FPU 8.5.1). A primitive is always ready when it is read: the response the main processor finds after an operand transfer is the next one, not come-again. The extra time is all *between* bus cycles, in RD68021's microcode:

| Where | Clocks in RD68021 | Example (`FADD.X (A0),FP1`) |
|---|---|---|
| Reading an evaluate-effective-address primitive, to the first operand read | 18 | response `$960C` ends at 1951, operand read at 1969 |
| Each long word of operand: memory read, operand-CIR write | 12 | 1969, 1981, 1993 |
| The last operand write, to the next response read | 7 | 2000 to 2007 |
| A null primitive, to the next instruction's first bus cycle | 9 to 15 | |

With a real MC68020 those rows would take the manual's protocol times plus RD68884's computation. That cannot be measured here, because the only MC68020 in this repository is RD68021.

| **Packed decimal in** | 0.13 to 0.29 of the manual | faster | One division, by 10^17 or 10^16 here: the operands are 7.5E-1 and 3E0. Across the range, on the ISS, packed input takes 150 to 220 core clocks: 1.5E300 takes 165, two multiplications by constant-ROM entries. |
| **FMOVE.P out** | 0.31 of the manual | faster | The k-factor scaling is the same single multiplication or division. The 17 digits are then divided out one at a time, a quotient bit a clock. |
| **Extended out** | +24 | slower | Three long words, at RD68021's rate. |
| **Control registers, FMOVEM** | +13 to +90 | slower | All protocol (the FPU ×30 count is within a few clocks): about 12 clocks per long word in RD68021, and 9 to 15 after each primitive. FMOVEM.X moves three long words per register. |
| **Conditionals** | +6 to +18; a taken FTRAP and its RTE +49 | slower | The condition is answered at once. The rest is RD68021's handling of the null primitive and, for FTRAP, its exception entry and RTE: RD68021's own `doc/timing-divergences.md` has TRAP #0 at +13 and RTE at +10 against an MC68020. |
| **FSAVE** | +27 null, +64 idle | slower | The first read of the save CIR is always answered come-again: the BIU answers that CIR at once and the frame's format word comes from the microcode (`doc/microcode.md`). Holding DSACK on the save CIR until the microcode posts the word, as the response CIR already does, would remove the come-again. That is a BIU change, not made. |
| **FRESTORE** | +7 null, +47 idle | slower | RD68021: per long word of frame, and the read-back of the restore CIR. |

## The measurement

`tools/cycles.py gen` writes the program, `build/programs/cycles.S`. Every row is:

```
        <setup>                     operands into FP0 and FP1, A0 at the row's data
        fnop                        the FPU idle, as FPU 8.1 assumes
        .balignw 4,0x4E71           the timed code long-word aligned
        move.l  CNT,%d6             sys_tb's clock count, at RES+$88
        <the instruction>
        fnop                        waits for whatever the instruction released
        move.l  CNT,%d7
```

The row's count is D7 − D6 less the same with no instruction (the first row). The FNOP after the instruction is what puts a released computation's tail in the count: Table 8-2's times are the whole instruction, the part the MC68881 lets the main processor overlap included (FPU 8.2). Two other choices keep the counts stable:
- The alignment keeps each row's count independent of where the row lands. Shifting the whole program by a word moves no row.
- RD68021's instruction cache stays off, as Table 8-2 assumes: "instruction prefetches do not hit in the MC68020 cache (or it is disabled)" (FPU 8.5.1).

The operands are the "typical" ones of FPU 8.5.1: normal and in range. The source is 0.75, with 3 for FSCALE, FMOD and FREM, 2.75 for FINT and FINTRZ, and 3 in the integer column. FP1 is 1.75, or 100.25 for FMOD and FREM. The integer column of FACOS, FASIN and FATANH is left out, because no integer is in their domain but 0 and ±1. FMOVE out stores 3.140625, packed with k = 17.

The manual's numbers were read from the page images, not the text layer, whose OCR is unreliable for numerals.

`sys_tb +trace` prints every bus cycle with its first and last clock, and `tools/cycles.py gen OUT.S --only TEXT` writes a program with only the matching rows. Together they were how the table above was explained.
